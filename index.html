This demo illustrates how a single portal can consolidate opportunity intake, profile management, scheduling, compliance gating, and payment tracking — while preserving internal operational control and override capability.

The Problem

Expert workflows are fragmented across email, documents, and manual coordination

Current state

Opportunity outreach happens via email with no structured response tracking. Experts may miss invitations or lack context to respond quickly.

With portal

Opportunities appear in a unified workspace with full brief, compliance flags, and screening questions — enabling sub-5-minute response.

Current state

Profile information decays silently. There is no systematic signal for when expertise data becomes stale or unreliable.

With portal

Profile freshness is tracked explicitly with recency signals, confidence scores, and re-confirmation prompts within a 90-day window.

Current state

Scheduling requires manual back-and-forth over email, often delayed by timezone confusion and missing availability data.

With portal

Experts define availability rules with timezone and lead-time preferences. Scheduling is gated on compliance completion.

Current state

Payment status is opaque to experts, generating support tickets and eroding trust after completed engagements.

With portal

Payment status is visible in the expert pipeline — pending, approved, and paid states reduce support friction to near zero.

What the portal centralizes

Seven operational surfaces in a single expert experience

Opportunity clarity

Structured opportunity briefs with scope, rate, deadline, and compliance flags replace unstructured email outreach.

Profile freshness

Recency signals, confidence scores, and re-confirmation workflows keep expertise data current and reliable.

Availability control

Rule-based availability with timezone, lead-time, and blackout preferences replaces per-request scheduling negotiation.

Compliance visibility

Attestation, conflict-of-interest checks, and MNPI flags are surfaced before scheduling — not discovered after.

Project pipeline transparency

Experts see where each engagement stands — from invitation through completion — without needing to ask.

Payment clarity

Payment states — pending, approved, paid — are visible to experts, eliminating post-engagement support friction.

Operational control layer

Internal teams retain override capability, audit trails, and governance controls. Self-service does not mean uncontrolled.

Expert lifecycle

A clear path from opportunity to payment

Opportunity → Screening → Compliance → Scheduling → Consultation → Payment

Opportunity → Screening

Expert receives a structured brief with scope, rate, and compliance flags. Screening questions capture fit and availability before proceeding.

Compliance → Scheduling

Attestation, conflict-of-interest checks, and MNPI declarations must be completed before the expert can be scheduled. This gate is enforced, not advisory.

Consultation → Payment

After the consultation is completed, payment transitions through pending, approved, and paid states — all visible to the expert in their pipeline view.

Design decisions

Why the product is structured this way

Compliance before scheduling. Attestation and conflict-of-interest checks are enforced gates, not optional steps. An expert cannot be scheduled until compliance is cleared — this prevents downstream risk and manual remediation.

Profile freshness is explicit. Rather than assuming profile data remains valid indefinitely, the system tracks recency signals with confidence scores and prompts re-confirmation within a 90-day window.

Payment visibility is included. Experts see payment status (pending, approved, paid) to reduce post-engagement support tickets. Transparency builds trust without exposing internal financial operations.

Availability as rules, not dates. Experts define recurring windows with timezone and lead-time preferences rather than picking individual slots. This reduces coordination overhead while keeping a conservative v1 boundary.

Ops override preserved. The operations layer retains full override capability for exceptions and governance. Self-service empowers experts but does not remove internal control.

AI conservative by design. AI-assisted features (matching, screening analysis) are scoped to assist, not automate. Human judgment remains the final decision layer for compliance and scheduling.
